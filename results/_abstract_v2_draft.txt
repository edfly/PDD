Spiking graph neural networks (GNNs) promise event-driven efficiency, yet the literature sets firing rates heuristically, conflates geometric and spiking gains with capacity differences, and reports energy as if hardware-measured. We argue the field's next step is evaluation methodology, not another accuracy or energy claim, and contribute one in three pillars: (A) capacity-matched separability, isolating gains from parameter budget; (B) firing-rate controllability, the commanded rate reported and reproduced via a rate-transfer ratio; (C) honest energy accounting, grading every claim Tier\,1 (analytic) or Tier\,2 (hardware-measured). We instantiate the discipline in SpikeRPGNN, whose neurons evolve on a hyperbolic--Euclidean product manifold, the firing rate a commanded constraint relaxed by $\mathcal{L}_{sr}=\lambda_{sr}(\bar{r}-r^{*})^{2}$. The honest findings: (i) the realized rate is a selectable, reproducible design parameter (56 of 60 runs land in the target band $[0.3,0.5]$), the contribution the method actually makes; (ii) the manifold is a substrate, not an accuracy device --- a small, non-significant accuracy contributor at matched capacity --- and, with the $n{=}5$ detection floor at roughly four paired standard deviations, non-significant comparisons are read as inconclusive, not null; (iii) the analytic energy--accuracy frontier (Proposition~5.2) is a diagnostic, not the training objective, and all energy figures are Tier\,1 estimates that straddle zero under $\pm50\%$ constant perturbations and reverse into an overrun on neuromorphic silicon. A failure-mode taxonomy and structural guards accompany the release. The advance is the discipline, not a Pareto win: it pins capacity, realized rate, and energy tier so later studies can line up against these numbers.
